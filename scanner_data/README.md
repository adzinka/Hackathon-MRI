# Scanner data pack: for the team members who know MRI hardware

**Why you are getting this.** Our digital twin follows a brain between two MRI sessions (May and November 2026).
The follow-up spectrum is worse: SNR 31.2 → 24.8, NAA linewidth 8.7 → 11.4 Hz. The brain twin cannot tell on its own
whether the **patient** changed or the **machine** did. We want a second twin, of the scanner itself, that answers:

> **When a follow-up scan looks different: is it the patient or the machine, why, and what should we do next?**

We need your expertise to make the scanner side physically right. Please read the five files below and answer the
questions at the end. Everything is regenerated by `scripts/export_scanner_data.py`. Do not edit the CSV files by hand;
send corrections as answers, and we change the generator.

## What is real and what is simulated

| File | Origin | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `real_session_qc.csv` | **REAL** (StarterPack v0.3) | Scanner-related QC of both sessions: SNR and linewidth as stated in the packets **and recomputed by us from the raw NIfTI-MRS files**, registration error, system-QC and calibration ids, protocol match, chemical-shift displacement |
| `real_acquisition.json` | **REAL** | PRESS at TE 30 ms, TR 2 s, 64 averages, 123.25 MHz (= 2.895 T, although labelled 3.0 T), VOI centre and size, ROI registration |
| `phantom_weekly_stable.csv` | **SIMULATED** | 27 weekly phantom checks, flat |
| `phantom_weekly_shim_degrading.csv` | **SIMULATED** | phantom linewidth rises from mid-September, SNR falls with it |
| `phantom_weekly_b0_drift.csv` | **SIMULATED** | centre frequency walks ~1.6 Hz/week, shim and SNR stable |
| `scanner_thresholds.json` | team **PROPOSAL** + StarterPack | our phantom gates, plus the scanner-related gates of `thresholds-v1.0` |

The StarterPack contains **no phantom record**. The packets only point to one (`system_qc_id = sysqc-synthetic-20261120`,
`calibration_id = phantom-2026-11`). Every phantom number here was invented by us to show how the method works, and it
is labelled SIMULATED wherever it appears.

Each phantom CSV has, per week and per observable: the raw value, its gate status (PASS / WARN / FAIL), and the
scanner-twin estimate `twin_x ± twin_sd`. That estimate uses the same gated Kalman step as the brain twin:
WARN counts at half weight, FAIL is rejected.

## Our draft: fault signatures (please correct this)

The "why" of a scanner error comes from **which observables move together**. This is our draft, from Dossier §5.2–5.4:

| Fault class | Phantom linewidth | Phantom SNR | Centre frequency | Geometry / distortion | Effect on the brain data |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shim / B0 homogeneity degraded | ↑ | ↓ (lower peak height) | – | – | broader MRS lines, Cho/Cr overlap, our WARN |
| B0 drift (cryostat, temperature) | ↑ within a scan if not corrected | – / ↓ | ↑↓ trend | – | peaks shift; smearing over 64 averages |
| RF receive chain / coil element | – | ↓ | – | – | lower SNR only, linewidth normal |
| RF transmit / B1 calibration | – | ↓ | – | – | flip-angle bias, contrast and quantification bias |
| Gradient calibration / non-linearity | – | – | – | ↑ | wrong distances and volumes, voxel misplacement |
| Positioning / registration (not the machine) | – | – | – | – (phantom fine) | ROI volume artefact, tissue exchange in the voxel |
| Patient (true change, motion) | – (phantom fine) | – | – | – | the only case where the brain itself changed |

Our November data show **linewidth ↑ and SNR ↓ together**. If the phantom showed the same, the first row is the
best explanation. If the phantom were flat, the cause would be in the session: local shim, head position or motion.

## Questions for you (please answer by the next team check-in)

1. **Phantom observables.** Are linewidth, SNR and centre frequency the right weekly observables? What would you add
   (ghosting, geometric distortion of a grid phantom, B1 map, temperature)?
2. **Realistic values.** For a spherical water phantom at 3 T, are ~6.8 Hz linewidth, SNR ~215 and ±2 Hz week-to-week
   frequency scatter realistic? What are typical values and repeatability?
3. **Thresholds.** Our proposal: linewidth WARN > 8 Hz, FAIL > 10 Hz; SNR WARN < 180, FAIL < 150; frequency WARN > 40 Hz,
   FAIL > 80 Hz. What would a site physicist use?
4. **Drift (Q).** How much does each observable drift in six months on a healthy scanner? This sets the twin's process noise.
5. **Signature table.** Is the table above right? Which faults look alike (and so cannot be told apart from phantom data alone)?
6. **Phantom vs brain linewidth.** Does a 2 Hz rise in phantom linewidth plausibly explain a 2.7 Hz rise in a frontal
   brain voxel (8.7 → 11.4 Hz)? How much of the in-vivo linewidth is subject-specific (susceptibility near the sinuses)?
7. **Frequency.** 123.25 MHz gives 2.895 T, not 3.0 T. Is a 3.5 % gap between label and measured field a fault or normal?
8. **Next measurement.** For each fault class, what is the cheapest check or fix (re-shim, frequency adjustment, coil
   test, gradient calibration with a grid phantom)? How many minutes does each take?
9. **What we must not claim.** Which statements would a physicist reject? We do not certify devices.

appears in the brain twin's competing explanations and colours the scanner bore in the 3D map (tab ⑤).

Our goal

Prove when a number is allowed to change, and by how much

The organisers do not want a brain-scanner app, a pretty 3D model or an AI that “finds disease”. They want one honest chain: a measurement comes in, we decide whether to trust it, we update what we believe, and we say what to measure next. Every step must be explainable.

Like a kid

You weigh your dog every month. One day the scale says 2 kg more. Did the dog get fat? Or was the scale on the carpet? Or did the dog have its collar on? Before you write the new weight in the dog’s notebook, you check. If you are unsure, you write it lightly in pencil. Next time you weigh it more carefully.

1 · Can we trust it?The evidence gate gives PASS, WARN or FAIL, with a reason and an owner track for every finding.
2 · How much should we move?The gain K says how far between “what we believed” and “what we just measured” we go. FAIL means we do not move.
3 · What next?Pick the measurement that removes the most doubt per scanner minute, or that removes the cause of a WARN.

What we are looking for in the data

The organisers hid one ambiguity in each follow-up packet. Our job is to find it and say which explanation is most likely. The dossier names four possible explanations for any change, and they can be mixed:

True changeThe object itself really changed.
Measurement variationNoise, a worse shim, a slightly different position.
Processing variationRegistration, segmentation or fitting software behaved differently.
Model discrepancyOur twin’s assumptions (for example the drift Q) are wrong.

Success criterion from the dossier: the team can defend why the twin changed, what did not change, and what should be measured next. The judges assess the explanation, not the amount of code.

What is a digital twin?

A notebook of beliefs that updates itself from evidence

A digital twin is a computer copy of what we know about a real thing. It is not a photo of it. It keeps a few important numbers about the thing, says how sure it is about each one, and updates them every time a new trustworthy measurement arrives. It can also predict and suggest what to do next.

Like a kid

A weather forecast is a kind of twin of the sky. It never sees the whole sky. It gets measurements from thermometers and satellites, keeps a best guess with “70 % chance of rain”, updates every hour, and tells you to take an umbrella.

So is the 3D brain our digital twin?

No. The 3D brain image is evidence: one of the photos the twin learns from. Our twin is much smaller. It is three numbers with their uncertainties, plus the rule that updates them.

Not a twin on its own

  • a 3D model or MRI image
  • a dashboard of charts
  • a patient record or a folder of scans
  • an AI classifier that labels images

The dossier says this directly: none of these is automatically a twin.

Our twin

  • ROI volume (size of one brain region): 22 190 ± 697 mm³
  • NAA/Cr (a chemical ratio): 1.227 ± 0.058
  • Cho/Cr (another chemical ratio): 0.741 ± 0.048
  • plus a gate, an update rule, a prediction and a next-measurement advisor

Why the 3D brain still matters

The chemistry comes from one small cubeCho/Cr is measured only inside a 20 × 20 × 20 mm cube in the frontal lobe at (−16, 45, 6) mm. The 3D view shows which tissue the cube covers. A different mix of grey matter, white matter and fluid changes the ratio without any real chemical change.
Position errors are 3D errorsA 2 mm shift of the cube swaps about 10 % of the tissue inside it. Even in one scan, the Cho and NAA cubes sit 2.4 mm apart (chemical-shift displacement).
The volume is built from 3D masksThe follow-up T1w image shows the same anatomy as the baseline, with only new noise. So the “+25 % growth” of the ROI is a processing artifact, and the images prove it.

What is “noise” in an MRI machine?

Trying to hear a whisper in a busy room

An MRI scanner listens to tiny radio signals sent out by hydrogen nuclei in the body. The signal is extremely weak. In a 3 T magnet only about 10 out of every million protons line up “extra” with the field. Those few make the whole signal. Metabolites like choline are also about 10 000 times rarer than water.

At the same time, everything warm jiggles: the salty water in the body and the electronics in the receiver coil. That random jiggling makes a random radio hiss. This hiss is the noise. It is different every time, like static on a radio.

Like a kid

Imagine your friend whispers a number across a noisy classroom. You hear it wrong sometimes. If she whispers it 64 times and you take the average, the random mistakes cancel out. That is exactly why the scanner repeats the spectrum 64 times (“64 averages”). The noise shrinks by √64 = 8 times.

Three peaks: NAA at 2.01 ppm, creatine (Cr) at 3.03 ppm, choline (Cho) at 3.20 ppm. Change the number of averages and the quality of the shim, and watch SNR and linewidth, the two checks that gave us WARN.

Noise is random. Bias is not.

Averaging fixes random noise. It does not fix errors that push every repeat the same way. Those are biases, and they are the trickier part of our challenge. From the dossier’s measurement chain (§5.2), in plain words:

Cho/Cr: the headline number

Why WARN
  • SNR 24.8, limit 25 QE. Noisier than baseline (31.2).
  • Linewidth 11.4 Hz, limit 10 Hz Metrology. Broader than baseline (8.7 Hz): a poor shim.
Why it matters
Cho (3.20 ppm) and Cr (3.03 ppm) are close neighbours. Broad lines make them overlap, so the fit can mix them up. The fit’s own uncertainty (CRLB) is a best case and too optimistic here, so we double the variance in the gain. That is our declared team policy.
What it means
z = 2.05: the measurement changed a bit more than noise alone explains. d² = 3.66 < 3.84: for the twin, which expects some drift over six months, it is not surprising. We move a third of the way and ask for a better measurement.

ROI volume: the trap

22 140 → 27 810 mm³ (+25.6 %) · twin 22 190 ± 697 · K = 0.009
Why WARN
The packet calls itself PASS with 4 % uncertainty. But its mask was copied onto the new scan through a registration with 1.1 mm error. For a region of radius about 17.4 mm that alone means about 3 × 1.1 / 17.4 ≈ 19 %. Our rule roi_u_consistency Metrology catches it and carries the honest 19 % forward.
What it means
With 19 %, z = 1.07: no significant change. With the claimed 4 % it would have been z = 4.38, a fake discovery. The twin holds its value until the registration is audited.
Lesson
Never trust a stored status. Re-check it.

Can we make a twin of the MRI scanner itself?

Yes. The dossier suggests it directly.

The dossier says the challenge is about the “health” of any system over its lifetime, and names the scanner as an example: “the MRI scanner is itself such a device: its instrument checks and phantom calibrations form a longitudinal health record of the instrument” (§11.3, Appendix J.6). The intro slides list the same idea: phantom frequency and SNR week by week, a drift budget, and “service before the images degrade”.

Like a kid

A doctor checks your health. But who checks the doctor’s thermometer? Every week the hospital puts a bottle with known liquid (a phantom) in the scanner and measures it. The bottle never changes. So if the numbers change, the machine changed. A twin of the scanner is the notebook of the machine’s health.

What its state would be

  • Center frequency ν₀: is the magnet’s field drifting? (now 123.25 MHz)
  • Phantom SNR: is the receiver chain getting noisier?
  • Phantom linewidth: how well can the scanner be shimmed?
  • Geometric distortion: are the gradients still accurate?
  • Context: room temperature, coil, software version

Same code: new observables in the packets, a new Q for each, and the same gate and Kalman step.

How it would help the brain twin

  • Separates “brain changed” from “machine changed”. If the scanner’s linewidth got worse between May and November, the Cho/Cr WARN is explained by the machine.
  • Feeds the gate. Every packet already carries system_qc_id. A scanner twin in FAIL would block patient updates automatically.
  • Predicts service. If drift trends upward, fix the machine before images get worse.
  • Shows federation. Two twins exchanging only evidence under a data contract is exactly the “federated architecture” of the intro lecture.

Limits to say out loud

  • The StarterPack has no phantom time series yet (the dossier lists the phantom tier as open). We would generate one ourselves and label it SIMULATED.
  • We may not certify a device. Our scanner twin only advises (“calibrate soon”), like the brain twin.
  • Real values for drift and repeatability must come from the site’s QC records, not from us.

Suggestion: this is a strong option for Gate 2 (“a second system”). It is cheap with our code, it connects all four tracks, and it is the transfer case the pitch template asks for in the last 30 seconds.

From the System Thinking slides (TU/e, 2IN70)

Why → How → What, and the black box

These slides are a way to think about any engineered system before building it. Main ideas, applied to our twin:

Why (cause, need)Doctors and engineers need to know whether a change is real before acting on it. False alarms and missed changes both cost a lot.
How (idea, form)Evidence packets + quality gate + Kalman update + next-measurement advisor. Our architecture.
What (existence)The working code, tests, the Streamlit app and the pitch.

The slides follow Plato’s path from cause to idea to existence and use TRIZ, a method for inventive problem solving: understand the problem, model it, find the contradictions, generate ideas, select, build and operate. MBSE (model-based systems engineering) is the toolbox for doing it with models instead of documents.

Our twin as a black box

A black box shows what goes in, what comes out, and what surrounds the system. It makes a good architecture slide.

Contradictions we solved (TRIZ language)

Use WARN data or ignore it?Ignoring wastes information; trusting it fully is dangerous. Our solution: use it, with doubled variance and the reasons shown.
More averages or less scanner time?Doubling averages costs time and still keeps the broad line. Re-shimming first removes the cause.
Detect change early or avoid false alarms?Two separate tests: z for the measurement, d² for the twin. A borderline case gets “update with warning” plus a repeat.

Glossary

MRI / MRSPictures from magnetic resonance / a chemical “fingerprint” of one small region.
PRESSThe scan recipe that picks the 20 mm cube and records its spectrum.
ppmPosition on the spectrum axis. Each molecule sings at its own ppm.
NAA, Cr, ChoThree molecules in the brain. Cr is used as the stable reference.
SNRSignal-to-noise ratio: peak height divided by noise. Higher is better.
Linewidth (FWHM)Peak width in Hz. Broad peaks overlap.
ShimTuning the magnetic field to be uniform before measuring.
PhantomA test object with known contents, scanned to check the machine.
CRLBThe smallest error a fit can have in theory. Real errors are usually bigger.
VOI / ROIThe spectroscopy cube / a region drawn on the brain image.
RegistrationAligning one scan onto another.
Provenance / SHA-256Where a number came from / a fingerprint proving a file is unchanged.
SIMULATED / MEASUREDLabels that say whether a number came from a computer or a real instrument. Never mix them.
